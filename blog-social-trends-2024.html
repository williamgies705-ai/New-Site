5 Social Media Trends Ontario Businesses Can’t Ignore

The platforms change quickly, but the fundamentals remain simple: earn attention, build trust and make the next step easy.

By Bill Gies · 5 minute read

1. Short video leads the feed

Short, useful videos remain one of the best ways for a local business to demonstrate expertise. A contractor can show a quick repair, a restaurant can reveal a finished dish and a professional service can answer one common customer question. Keep the opening direct and make the value obvious within the first few seconds.

2. Real beats overly polished

Customers want to see the people, process and standards behind a business. Clean production matters, but content should still feel human. Mix polished campaign pieces with jobsite footage, team introductions and short explanations recorded in the real working environment.

3. Search happens inside social apps

People increasingly use social platforms to discover local services. Include your city, service area and the plain-language terms customers actually search. A clear caption such as “roof repair in Barrie” is more useful than a vague slogan.

4. Community creates reach

Reply to comments, acknowledge local partners and participate in relevant community conversations. Consistent interaction helps turn an audience into recognition. It also signals that a real business is active behind the account.

5. Every post needs a job

Before publishing, decide whether the post should create awareness, build trust or generate an inquiry. Then use one clear call to action. Asking people to call, request a quote and visit three different links at once weakens the message.

BrandSpring takeawayChoose two repeatable video formats, publish them consistently for one month and measure inquiries—not just likes.
